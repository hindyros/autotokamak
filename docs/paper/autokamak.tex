\large A Mathematical System Description}
\author{Hindy Rossignol}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\emph{Autokamak} is a research platform that couples a finite-element
Grad--Shafranov (GS) equilibrium solver (\textsc{TokaMaker}/OpenFUSIONToolkit)
with a machine-learning pipeline that learns fast surrogate models for the
poloidal flux field $\psi(R,Z)$, and with an LLM-driven \emph{meta-agent}
that plans and runs the pipeline end to end. The system is organized in three
phases: (i) generation of a seed dataset by space-filling sampling of tokamak
geometries; (ii) automated surrogate training with dimensionality reduction
and hyperparameter search; and (iii) \emph{residual-driven active sampling},
in which a Gaussian process learns \emph{where the current surrogate is wrong}
as a function of geometry and proposes the next equilibria to solve. An outer
meta-loop selects among data-enrichment and re-tuning actions until a
performance target is met. This document gives a self-contained mathematical
account of every model in the pipeline, stating each optimization problem
explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{System Overview}
% ===========================================================================

We wish to approximate the map from a low-dimensional description of a plasma
equilibrium to the full two-dimensional flux field it produces. Let
\begin{equation}
  \x = \paramvec \in \X \subset \R^5
\end{equation}
denote the \emph{geometry vector}: major radius $R_0$, minor radius $a$,
elongation $\kappa$, triangularity $\delta$, and total plasma current $I_p$.
The ground-truth solver returns the poloidal flux $\psi(R,Z)$, which after
interpolation onto a fixed $n_z\times n_r$ grid we regard as a vector
$\psiv \in \R^{D}$ with $D = n_z n_r$ (in the shipped configuration
$n_z=96$, $n_r=64$, so $D=6144$). The object we learn is the operator
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}:\ \X \to \R^{D}, \qquad \x \mapsto \psiv,
\end{equation}
called the \emph{surrogate}. The three phases of the pipeline correspond to:
building a training set $\{(\x_i,\psiv_i)\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:phase1}); fitting
$\mathcal{S}$ (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}); and choosing new $\x$ to solve so as
to improve $\mathcal{S}$ where it is weakest (Section~\ref{sec:phase3}). An
LLM meta-agent orchestrates the loop (Section~\ref{sec:meta}), and an agentic
code-generation layer (URSA) authors open-ended pipeline components
(Section~\ref{sec:ursa}).

% ===========================================================================
\section{The Ground-Truth Problem: Grad--Shafranov Equilibrium}
\label{sec:gs}
% ===========================================================================

A magnetically confined, axisymmetric plasma in force balance satisfies the
\emph{Grad--Shafranov equation}, a nonlinear elliptic PDE for the poloidal
flux $\psi(R,Z)$ in the poloidal plane $(R,Z)$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{\!*}\psi
  \;=\; -\,\mu_0 R^2\, p'(\psi)\;-\;F(\psi)\,F'(\psi),
  \label{eq:gs}
\end{equation}
where the elliptic \emph{Shafranov} operator is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{\!*}\psi
  \;=\; R\,\frac{\partial}{\partial R}\!\left(\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial R}\right)
  + \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial Z^2}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mu_0$ is the vacuum permeability, $p(\psi)$ the plasma pressure profile,
and $F(\psi)=R B_\phi$ the poloidal-current function; primes denote
$\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}\psi$. The right-hand side is the toroidal current density
$-\mu_0 R\, J_\phi$. Equation~\eqref{eq:gs} is solved on the plasma
cross-section bounded by the \emph{last closed flux surface} (LCFS)
$\lcfs$, a closed curve prescribed (in the fixed-boundary setting used here)
by the shaping parameters through a Miller-type parameterization
\begin{equation}
  R(\theta) = R_0 + a\cos\!\big(\theta + \arcsin\delta \cdot \sin\theta\big),
  \qquad
  Z(\theta) = z_0 + \kappa\, a \sin\theta,
  \qquad \theta \in [0,2\pi).
  \label{eq:lcfs}
\end{equation}
\textsc{TokaMaker} discretizes~\eqref{eq:gs} with a finite-element method on an
unstructured triangular mesh of the region enclosed by $\lcfs$, imposing the
total current constraint $\int J_\phi \,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}Z = I_p$ and an
isoflux boundary condition matching $\lcfs$. The nonlinearity in $p'$ and
$FF'$ is resolved by Picard iteration. The solver is the \emph{oracle}: given
$\x$, it returns $\psi$ on the mesh, which we bilinearly interpolate to the
fixed $(R,Z)$ grid to obtain $\psiv \in \R^{D}$. Cells outside $\lcfs$ are
undefined and marked \texttt{NaN}. A solve occasionally fails (mesh
degeneracy or non-convergence at extreme shaping); we record a
\emph{feasibility} indicator $y^{\mathrm{feas}} \in \{0,1\}$ per attempt,
which becomes important in Section~\ref{sec:phase3}.

Each solve costs seconds of wall-clock; a surrogate that emulates
$\mathcal{S}$ in microseconds is the goal, and the cost asymmetry is what
makes \emph{which equilibria to solve} an economically meaningful question.

\section{Phase 1: Seed Dataset Generation}
\label{sec:phase1}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Sampling designs}

Phase~1 draws $N$ geometries from a bounded box
$\mathcal{B} = \prod_{d=1}^5 [\ell_d, u_d] \subseteq \X$ (the \emph{seed box})
and solves each. Because we cannot yet know where the eventual surrogate will
struggle, the seed draw is \emph{blind}; the design goal is uniform coverage.
Three seeded designs are supported.

\paragraph{Uniform.} Independent draws $\x_i \sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal{B})$.
Simple, but at finite $N$ leaves clusters and gaps by chance.

\paragraph{Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS).} Partition each axis into $N$ equal
bins and permute so that every bin is occupied exactly once per dimension.
With $\pi^{(d)}$ a random permutation of $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and
$U^{(d)}_i \sim \mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
  x_{i,d} = \ell_d + (u_d-\ell_d)\,\frac{\pi^{(d)}_i + U^{(d)}_i}{N}.
\end{equation}
LHS guarantees one-dimensional uniformity by construction.

\paragraph{Sobol (low-discrepancy).} A deterministic, scrambled Sobol sequence
minimizes the \emph{star discrepancy}
\begin{equation}
  D^{\star}_N \;=\; \sup_{\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}}
  \left|\,\frac{\#\{\x_i \in [\ell,\bm{b})\}}{N}
  - \frac{\mathrm{vol}[\ell,\bm{b})}{\mathrm{vol}\,\mathcal{B}}\,\right|,
\end{equation}
which measures the worst-case deviation between the empirical fraction of
points in any sub-box and its volume fraction. Low-discrepancy sequences
achieve $D^\star_N = O\!\big((\log N)^5/N\big)$ versus $O(N^{-1/2})$ for i.i.d.
uniform, giving the most even coverage of $\mathcal{B}$ in five dimensions.
The same scrambled-Sobol routine supplies the acquisition candidate pool in
Section~\ref{sec:phase3}.

\subsection{Dataset}
Solving the $N$ designs yields the seed dataset
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{D}_N = \big\{(\x_i,\,\psiv_i,\,y^{\mathrm{feas}}_i)\big\}_{i=1}^{N},
\end{equation}
where $\psiv_i$ is defined only for feasible solves. We store \emph{physical}
$\psi$ in webers (not the normalized $\psi_N\in[0,1]$), because normalization
erases the $I_p$ amplitude dependence the surrogate must learn.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Phase 2: Surrogate Modeling}
\label{sec:phase2}
% ===========================================================================

Direct regression onto $\R^{D}$ ($D\approx 6144$) from five inputs is
ill-posed. Phase~2 therefore factors $\mathcal{S}$ into a linear
dimensionality reduction learned from data and a low-dimensional nonlinear
regressor:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}(\x) \;=\; \bm{\mu}_\psi + \Wmat\, h(\x),
  \label{eq:surrogate-factor}
\end{equation}
with $\bm{\mu}_\psi\in\R^D$ a mean field, $\Wmat\in\R^{D\times k}$ an
orthonormal basis, and $h:\X\to\R^k$ the regressor ($k\ll D$).

\subsection{Principal Component Analysis of the flux field}

Let $\bm{\mu}_\psi = \frac1n\sum_i \psiv_i$ (per-pixel mean; \texttt{NaN} cells
are mean-filled). PCA seeks the rank-$k$ orthonormal basis minimizing
reconstruction error, equivalently maximizing retained variance:
\begin{equation}
  \Wmat^\star
  = \argmin_{\Wmat\in\R^{D\times k},\;\Wmat^\top\Wmat=\Imat_k}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n} \big\|\,(\psiv_i-\bm{\mu}_\psi)
  - \Wmat\Wmat^\top(\psiv_i-\bm{\mu}_\psi)\,\big\|_2^2 .
  \label{eq:pca}
\end{equation}
The solution is the matrix whose columns are the top-$k$ eigenvectors of the
empirical covariance
$\bm{\Sigma}=\frac1n\sum_i(\psiv_i-\bm{\mu}_\psi)(\psiv_i-\bm{\mu}_\psi)^\top$,
ordered by eigenvalue $\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_k$. Each field is encoded by
its coefficient vector and reconstructed by
\begin{equation}
  \cvec_i = \Wmat^{\star\top}(\psiv_i-\bm{\mu}_\psi)\in\R^k,
  \qquad
  \hat{\psiv}_i = \bm{\mu}_\psi + \Wmat^\star \cvec_i .
\end{equation}
The retained-variance fraction is
$\mathrm{EV}(k)=\sum_{j\le k}\lambda_j / \sum_{j}\lambda_j$; we choose the
smallest $k$ with $\mathrm{EV}(k)\ge 0.95$ (physical $\psi$ typically needs
$k\approx 8$). The regression target is thus the compact $\cvec\in\R^k$, not
the $6144$-dimensional field.

\subsection{The regressor zoo}

The regressor $h:\X\to\R^k$ is chosen from four classical families. All
operate on standardized inputs $\tilde\x = (\x-\bar\x)/\bm{s}$ (component-wise;
$I_p\sim10^5$ dwarfs $R_0\sim10^{-1}$ otherwise). We state each family's
training objective; multi-output ($k$ coefficients) is handled per output or
jointly as noted.

\paragraph{(a) Polynomial ridge regression.}
With polynomial feature map $\phi_d(\x)$ of total degree $\le d$, each
coefficient channel $j$ solves a ridge least-squares problem
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\beta}_j^\star
  = \argmin_{\bm{\beta}\in\R^{m}}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(c_{i,j} - \bm{\beta}^\top\phi_d(\tilde\x_i)\big)^2
  + \alpha\,\|\bm{\beta}\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
with closed-form solution
$\bm{\beta}_j^\star=(\bm{\Phi}^\top\bm{\Phi}+\alpha\Imat)^{-1}\bm{\Phi}^\top\bm{c}_{:,j}$,
where $\bm{\Phi}$ stacks $\phi_d(\tilde\x_i)^\top$. Hyperparameters:
$\alpha$ (ridge penalty), $d\in\{1,2,3\}$.

\paragraph{(b) Kernel ridge regression (KRR).}
Working in a reproducing-kernel Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
  f_j^\star = \argmin_{f\in\mathcal{H}_\kappa}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(c_{i,j}-f(\tilde\x_i)\big)^2 + \lambda\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_\kappa}^2 .
\end{equation}
By the representer theorem the minimizer is
$f_j^\star(\x)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\,\kappa(\x,\x_i)$ with dual weights
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\alpha}_j = (\Kmat + \lambda \Imat)^{-1}\,\bm{c}_{:,j},
  \qquad K_{ii'}=\kappa(\tilde\x_i,\tilde\x_{i'}),
\end{equation}
and kernel $\kappa$ chosen from RBF $\kappa(\x,\x')=\exp(-\gamma\|\x-\x'\|^2)$
or Laplacian $\exp(-\gamma\|\x-\x'\|_1)$. Hyperparameters: $\lambda$ (=\,\texttt{alpha}),
$\gamma$, kernel type.

\paragraph{(c) Gaussian process regression (GPR).}
Place a GP prior $f\sim\gp(0,\kappa_\vartheta)$ and Gaussian likelihood
$c = f(\tilde\x)+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\Normal(0,\sigma_n^2)$.
Kernel hyperparameters $\vartheta$ (signal variance, length scale, noise) are
fit by maximizing the log marginal likelihood
\begin{equation}
  \vartheta^\star = \argmax_{\vartheta}\;
  -\tfrac12\bm{c}^\top(\Kmat_\vartheta+\sigma_n^2\Imat)^{-1}\bm{c}
  -\tfrac12\log\big|\Kmat_\vartheta+\sigma_n^2\Imat\big|
  -\tfrac{n}{2}\log 2\pi .
  \label{eq:gp-mll}
\end{equation}
Prediction at a test $\x_*$ is Gaussian with the standard posterior mean/variance
(Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gp-post-mean}--\eqref{eq:gp-post-var} below, applied to the
$\cvec$ targets). Hyperparameters exposed to search: \texttt{length\_scale},
\texttt{noise\_level}, \texttt{alpha} (jitter).

\paragraph{(d) Multilayer perceptron (MLP).}
A feed-forward network $f_\theta$ (at most two hidden layers, width $\le256$)
minimizes penalized mean-squared error
\begin{equation}
  \theta^\star=\argmin_\theta
  \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big\|\cvec_i-f_\theta(\tilde\x_i)\big\|_2^2
  + \alpha\,\|\theta\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
optimized by Adam. Hyperparameters: depth, width, $\ell_2$ penalty $\alpha$,
initial learning rate. Deep operator/PINN/FNO families are deliberately out of
scope for this proof of concept.

\subsection{Model selection: cross-validated hyperparameter search}
\label{sec:optuna}

For a model family $M$ and hyperparameters $\bm{\eta}$, quality is the
$K$-fold cross-validated error measured in \emph{original} $\psi$ units (PCA is
refit on each training fold to avoid leakage, then inverse-transformed before
scoring):
\begin{equation}
  J(M,\bm{\eta})
  = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{f=1}^{K}
  \rmse\!\Big(\{\psiv_i\}_{i\in V_f},\,
  \{\bm{\mu}_\psi^{(f)}+\Wmat^{(f)} h_{M,\bm{\eta}}^{(f)}(\x_i)\}_{i\in V_f}\Big),
  \label{eq:cv-objective}
\end{equation}
where $V_f$ is validation fold $f$ and the superscript $(f)$ denotes quantities
fit on the complementary training rows. The RMSE over valid grid cells $\Omega$
is
\begin{equation}
  \rmse(\psiv,\hat\psiv)
  = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{(R,Z)\in\Omega}\big(\psi(R,Z)-\hat\psi(R,Z)\big)^2}.
  \label{eq:rmse}
\end{equation}
The search minimizes $J$ over the joint (family, hyperparameter) space using the
Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE), a sequential model-based optimizer that
models $p(\bm{\eta}\mid J<J^\ast)$ and $p(\bm{\eta}\mid J\ge J^\ast)$ and samples
$\bm{\eta}$ maximizing their ratio:
\begin{equation}
  (M^\star,\bm{\eta}^\star) = \argmin_{M,\bm{\eta}} J(M,\bm{\eta}).
\end{equation}
Writing $\hat h = h_{M^\star,\bm{\eta}^\star}$ for the winner, the winning
configuration is refit on \emph{all} non-test rows to produce the deployed
payload $\{\Wmat,\bm{\mu}_\psi,\hat h\}$.

\subsection{Reference metric: skill over the trivial baseline}

To make errors interpretable and scale-free we compare against the trivial
predictor that ignores $\x$ and returns the per-pixel training mean
$\bm{\mu}_\psi$. Its error on an evaluation set is
\begin{equation}
  \rmse_{\mathrm{base}}
  = \rmse\!\big(\{\psiv_i\}_{i\in\mathrm{eval}},\,\{\bm{\mu}_\psi\}\big),
\end{equation}
and we define \emph{accuracy} as the fractional error reduction
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Acc} \;=\; 100\left(1-\frac{\rmse}{\rmse_{\mathrm{base}}}\right)\%.
  \label{eq:accuracy}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{Acc}=0$ means no better than the mean; $\mathrm{Acc}\to100$ means
near-perfect. This quantity drives the meta-loop stopping rule
(Section~\ref{sec:stopping}).

\section{Phase 3: Residual-Driven Active Sampling}
\label{sec:phase3}
% ===========================================================================

Given a trained surrogate, active sampling asks: \emph{which new geometries,
once solved, most improve the surrogate per solve spent?} Our answer is to
learn a probabilistic model of the surrogate's own error over geometry space
and sample where that error is large \emph{or} uncertain.

\subsection{Out-of-fold residuals: an honest error signal}

Let $\hat h$ be the current winner. Its residual must be measured
out-of-sample, because interpolating models (GPR, KRR) fit their own training
points to $\approx 0$ error. We therefore compute \emph{out-of-fold} (OOF)
residuals: partition the training pool into $K$ folds, refit the winner's
architecture on each complement, and score each sample on the fold that held it
out. For sample $i$ in fold $f(i)$,
\begin{equation}
  r_i \;=\; \frac{1}{|\Omega_i|}\sum_{(R,Z)\in\Omega_i}
  \big|\,\psi_i(R,Z)-\hat\psi_i^{(-f(i))}(R,Z)\,\big|,
  \label{eq:oof}
\end{equation}
the mean absolute flux error of the winner \emph{refit without} the fold
containing $i$. This $r_i$ is the empirical ``how wrong is the surrogate at
$\x_i$'' signal.

\subsection{A Gaussian process over the error surface}

We now learn the function $g:\X\to\R$ mapping geometry to (log) expected
surrogate error, treating $\{(\tilde\x_i,\,\log r_i)\}$ as noisy observations.
We place a GP prior
\begin{equation}
  g \sim \gp\big(0,\;\kappa_\vartheta\big),\qquad
  \log r_i = g(\tilde\x_i) + \varepsilon_i,\quad \varepsilon_i\sim\Normal(0,\sigma_n^2),
\end{equation}
with an \emph{automatic relevance determination} (ARD) squared-exponential
kernel plus a white-noise term,
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_\vartheta(\x,\x')
  = \sigma_f^2 \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12\sum_{d=1}^{5}
  \frac{(x_d-x'_d)^2}{\ell_d^2}\right) + \sigma_n^2\,\delta_{\x\x'} .
  \label{eq:ard}
\end{equation}
Per-dimension length scales $\ell_d$ let the model discover which geometry axes
drive error. The log target is used because error is multiplicative and
heavy-tailed; the log keeps a few catastrophic samples from dominating.

\paragraph{The GP is a distribution over functions.} Conditioning the prior on
the OOF data $\bm{y}=(\log r_1,\dots,\log r_m)^\top$ at inputs
$\tilde{X}=\{\tilde\x_i\}$ yields a \emph{posterior distribution over the whole
space of error-surfaces} $g$. It is characterized by the property that, at any
finite set of query geometries, the function values are jointly Gaussian. At a
single candidate $\x_*$,
\begin{align}
  \mu_g(\x_*)
  &= \bm{k}_*^\top(\Kmat+\sigma_n^2\Imat)^{-1}\bm{y},
  \label{eq:gp-post-mean}\\[2pt]
  \sigma_g^2(\x_*)
  &= \kappa_\vartheta(\x_*,\x_*) - \bm{k}_*^\top(\Kmat+\sigma_n^2\Imat)^{-1}\bm{k}_* ,
  \label{eq:gp-post-var}
\end{align}
where $K_{ii'}=\kappa_\vartheta(\tilde\x_i,\tilde\x_{i'})$ and
$[\bm{k}_*]_i=\kappa_\vartheta(\x_*,\tilde\x_i)$. Thus $\mu_g(\x_*)$ is the
posterior mean prediction of the surrogate's (log) error at $\x_*$, and
$\sigma_g(\x_*)$ its posterior standard deviation --- large where many plausible
error-surfaces are consistent with the data but disagree, i.e.\ where we have
not sampled. Hyperparameters $\vartheta=(\sigma_f,\{\ell_d\},\sigma_n)$ are fit
by maximizing the marginal likelihood~\eqref{eq:gp-mll} on the OOF targets.

\subsection{Feasibility model}

Solves fail in parts of $\X$, and a failed solve costs as much as a success.
We learn the probability of success with a GP \emph{classifier}: a latent GP
$\phi\sim\gp(0,\kappa)$ mapped through a sigmoid link,
\begin{equation}
  p(\x) \;=\; \Prob(y^{\mathrm{feas}}=1\mid \x)
  \;=\; \sigma\big(\phi(\x)\big),
  \qquad \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},
\end{equation}
fit on \emph{all} attempts (successes and failures) by Laplace approximation of
the posterior (the non-Gaussian likelihood has no closed form). We convert the
probability into an acquisition weight with an exploration floor
$\rho\in(0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
  w(\x) = \max\!\big(p(\x)^2,\ \rho\big).
  \label{eq:feas-weight}
\end{equation}
Squaring makes a known-bad region unable to out-bid a merely well-covered
feasible one on prior variance alone; the floor (default $\rho=0.02$) ensures no
region is written off permanently. Setting $\rho=1$ disables feasibility
weighting entirely.

\subsection{Acquisition: upper confidence bound}

We score candidate geometries by an upper-confidence-bound (UCB) rule that
trades exploitation of known-high error against exploration of error-model
uncertainty, modulated by feasibility. Working in log space (so the feasibility
weight enters additively),
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\;
  a(\x) \;=\; \underbrace{\mu_g(\x)}_{\text{expected error}}
  \;+\; \beta\,\underbrace{\sigma_g(\x)}_{\text{error-model uncertainty}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\log w(\x)}_{\text{feasibility}} .
  \;\;}
  \label{eq:ucb}
\end{equation}
The parameter $\beta\ge0$ (clamped to $[0,3]$, chosen by the meta-agent)
controls exploration: $\beta=0$ samples purely where the model is
\emph{predicted} worst; large $\beta$ samples where the error model is
\emph{uncertain}, which is precisely how the batch reaches unvisited regions of
a target envelope wider than the seed box. Equation~\eqref{eq:ucb} is the
GP-UCB acquisition function of Bayesian optimization, applied here to the
surrogate's \emph{error} rather than to $\psi$ itself.

\subsection{Batch selection by exact variance conditioning}

We must pick a \emph{batch} of $B$ points, not one, and they should not pile
onto a single peak. We select greedily from a Sobol candidate pool
$\mathcal{C}$ of size $N_c$ (default $4096$). A key fact: the GP posterior
variance~\eqref{eq:gp-post-var} depends only on input \emph{locations}, not on
observed values. Hence we can condition on a chosen point using a hallucinated
observation at its own posterior mean (the ``kriging believer'' heuristic):
the posterior mean is unchanged, and only the variance $\sigma_g^2$ shrinks near
the chosen point. After each pick we update the Cholesky factor of
$(\Kmat+\sigma_n^2\Imat)$ by a rank-one border update and re-score the pool.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:acq} summarizes the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Residual-driven UCB batch acquisition}
\label{alg:acq}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require winner $\hat h$; pool $\mathcal{D}$; bounds $[\bm\ell,\bm u]$; batch $B$; exploration $\beta$
\State compute OOF residuals $r_i$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:oof}; set targets $y_i=\log r_i$
\State fit error-model GP $g$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ard}) and feasibility model $p$; hyperparameters by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gp-mll}
\State draw Sobol candidate pool $\mathcal{C}=\{\x^{(1)},\dots,\x^{(N_c)}\}\subset[\bm\ell,\bm u]$
\State precompute $\mu_g(\x)$, $\log w(\x)$ for all $\x\in\mathcal{C}$
\State $\mathcal{A}\gets\varnothing$
\For{$b=1,\dots,B$}
  \State $\x^\star \gets \argmax_{\x\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{A}}\;
         \mu_g(\x)+\beta\,\sigma_g(\x)+\log w(\x)$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:ucb}}
  \State $\mathcal{A}\gets\mathcal{A}\cup\{\x^\star\}$
  \State condition $g$ on $\x^\star$ (kriging believer): rank-one Cholesky update, shrinking $\sigma_g^2$ near $\x^\star$
\EndFor
\State \Return batch $\mathcal{A}$ to solve with the oracle
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The returned batch is solved by the oracle, merged into the pool, and the
winner is refit --- yielding immediate credit (or none) on the frozen
evaluation set. When no winner yet exists (cold start) or data is too sparse,
the strategy degrades gracefully: to a model-agnostic PCA-variance acquisition
(a GP over $\psi$ coefficients, chasing predictive variance of the field
itself), and finally to feasibility-weighted maximin space-filling.

\subsection{Strategy selection}

The meta-agent may override the automatic ladder with one of four strategies:
\texttt{auto} (residual-driven if a winner exists, else uncertainty);
\texttt{residual\_ucb} (force Eq.~\eqref{eq:ucb}); \texttt{uncertainty}
(model-agnostic field variance, for when the winner's residuals are still
noisy); and \texttt{space\_filling} (pure maximin, to blanket a newly widened
region before targeting within it).

% ===========================================================================
\section{The Meta-Loop: Autonomous Orchestration}
\label{sec:meta}
% ===========================================================================

An outer loop drives Phases 1--3 as a sequential decision process. At each
iteration $t$ the meta-agent observes a state $s_t$ (diagnostics of the current
model: learning-curve slope, cross-seed variance, PCA spectrum, edge-hit flags,
per-input residual correlations) and selects an action
\begin{equation}
  a_t \in \{\texttt{regen\_dataset},\ \texttt{enrich\_active},\
  \texttt{extend\_search},\ \texttt{terminate}\},
\end{equation}
respectively: append a blind space-filling batch (control arm); append a
residual-driven active batch (Section~\ref{sec:phase3}); run another Phase-2
hyperparameter search; or stop. The action is produced by an LLM policy
$\pi_\Theta$ expressed as a structured program that emits a typed decision; a
deterministic dispatcher executes it. The policy \emph{prompt} $\Theta$ is
itself optimizable: given a dataset of (state, decision, downstream-score)
traces, a reflective prompt-evolution procedure (GEPA) searches
\begin{equation}
  \Theta^\star = \argmax_{\Theta}\;
  \E_{\tau\sim\pi_\Theta}\big[\,\mathrm{Score}(\tau)\,\big],
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Score}$ is a composite of hard gates (deliverables present and
loadable, report parseable) and weighted quality terms (final skill over
baseline, monotone improvement across iterations, budget efficiency,
non-wasted iterations, agent-chosen termination). Only the decision policy is
learned; the acquisition mathematics of Section~\ref{sec:phase3} is fixed and
deterministic for reproducibility.

\subsection{Evaluation over a target envelope}

Active sampling expands into geometries the seed box never covered, so the
evaluation set must represent the full \emph{target envelope}
$\mathcal{E}\supseteq\mathcal{B}$, not the seed distribution. We draw one
independent LHS eval set over $\mathcal{E}$, solve it once, and \emph{freeze}
it; every RMSE the loop reports is measured on these fixed samples. To reveal
\emph{where} the surrogate is weak we stratify $\mathcal{E}$ into a grid of
geometry cells $\{\mathcal{E}_q\}$ ($b$ bins per axis) and report per-cell
error
\begin{equation}
  \rmse_q = \rmse\!\big(\{\psiv_i\}_{i:\x_i\in\mathcal{E}_q},\ \{\mathcal{S}(\x_i)\}\big),
\end{equation}
with headline summaries $\max_q \rmse_q$ (worst cell) and mean-over-cells.
Aggregate RMSE alone cannot distinguish ``uniformly mediocre'' from ``excellent
in the seed box, broken at high $\kappa$'' --- exactly the distinction active
sampling exists to close.

\subsection{Performance stopping criteria}
\label{sec:stopping}

The loop runs at most $T_{\max}$ iterations but stops early once the model is
good enough. Three aggregate targets --- an absolute RMSE $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}$,
a ratio $\rho_r$ of baseline, and an accuracy $A$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:accuracy}) ---
are the same quantity in different units and collapse to the strictest
threshold
\begin{equation}
  \tau^\star = \min\Big\{\tau_{\mathrm{abs}},\;
  \rho_r\,\rmse_{\mathrm{base}},\;
  \big(1-\tfrac{A}{100}\big)\rmse_{\mathrm{base}}\Big\},
\end{equation}
and the loop halts at the first iteration with $\rmse \le \tau^\star$. A
stricter, per-region criterion halts only when \emph{every} occupied eval cell
clears an accuracy bar against \emph{its own} baseline,
\begin{equation}
  \min_{q}\;100\left(1-\frac{\rmse_q}{\rmse_{\mathrm{base},q}}\right)
  \;\ge\; A_{\mathrm{cell}} ,
\end{equation}
using per-cell baselines so a large-$\psi$ region is not penalized for its
scale. This ``good everywhere'' rule is the natural target for active learning:
it cannot be satisfied by a model that is accurate only in the seed box.

\section{The Agentic Code-Generation Layer (URSA)}
\label{sec:ursa}
% ===========================================================================

Beyond the fixed decision policy, an agentic layer (URSA) authors open-ended,
code-shaped pipeline components via a plan--execute--feedback loop: a planner
LLM emits a sequence of steps from a natural-language task; an executor LLM
writes and runs code for each; and, after execution, the planner re-plans given
the execution history for a bounded number of feedback rounds. This layer is
used where the task is genuinely open (rather than a small typed decision), most
notably \emph{representation search} --- exploring alternative input
featurizations and output transforms for the surrogate under a fixed evaluation
protocol --- and, optionally, as a code-generation backend for Phase-2 search.
It is the mechanism by which the platform can extend its own data-engineering
surface rather than only tuning within a fixed one.

\section{Summary}
% ===========================================================================

Autokamak composes four mathematical layers. A finite-element solver provides
ground truth for the Grad--Shafranov equation~\eqref{eq:gs}. A surrogate
factored as PCA~\eqref{eq:pca} plus a cross-validated
regressor~\eqref{eq:cv-objective} emulates the flux field. A Gaussian process
over the surrogate's out-of-fold residuals~\eqref{eq:oof}--\eqref{eq:gp-post-var}
turns ``where is the model wrong'' into a probabilistic surface, and a
UCB rule~\eqref{eq:ucb} converts it into the next equilibria to solve. An LLM
meta-policy sequences data enrichment and re-tuning against explicit accuracy
targets~\eqref{eq:accuracy}. The through-line is a single principle: because
the oracle is expensive and the surrogate is cheap, every design choice ---
sampling, reduction, model selection, and above all acquisition --- is aimed at
extracting the most model improvement per oracle call.
